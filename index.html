THE RL LOOP

RL does not memorize an answer. It makes good outcomes more likely.

STATEWhere am I?
ACTIONWhere next?
OUTCOMEWhat happened?
REWARDWas it good?
UPDATEPrefer it next time
POLICY“behavior habit”

Objective: maximize expected cumulative reward, not copy one canonical route.

Reveal the five elements in order. A policy is the agent's behavioral habit: which action it tends to choose in each state. We keep the loop and skip the Bellman equation. [Sources] Sutton & Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, 2nd ed. http://incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html

Timing: 1.5 minutes. “Advantage” means better or worse than a baseline. PPO typically estimates that baseline with a learned critic/value model. GRPO replaces it with statistics from the sampled group, reducing memory use. Exact implementations may normalize rewards in slightly different ways. [Source] Shao et al., DeepSeekMath. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300

GRPO · STEP 3 · 25:00

Increase good trajectories — but do not let one batch move the policy too far.

01 · DIRECTION

Positive advantage makes a trajectory more likely.

02 · CLIPPING

Limit how much the probability ratio can matter.

03 · KL PENALTY

Discourage drifting too far from a reference policy.

The undergraduate version: compare within a group, nudge probabilities, keep the update stable.

Timing: 2 minutes. Do not derive the objective. Point to three ideas: advantage gives direction, clipping limits the update, and a KL penalty discourages extreme drift. In full GRPO, the objective is evaluated at the token level across sampled completions. [Source] Shao et al., DeepSeekMath. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300

SFT often gives the model one reference response. RLVR can sample several responses from the current model, run tests, and make successful trajectories more likely. This reward sees the outcome, so it may miss style, safety, or maintainability.

THE BILL COMES DUE · 38:00

REWARD100
REAL QUALITY?

the model may learn a shortcut.

01Reward hacking

02Sparse reward

03Entropy collapse

Helff et al., 2026 · DAPO, 2025

RL does not decide whether an objective is what we truly want. It becomes better at optimizing the reward we gave it. RLVR verifiers can contain loopholes, and one final reward creates a credit-assignment problem over a long reasoning chain.
